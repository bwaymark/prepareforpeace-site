# Cooking at scale

In a crisis, a warm meal shared with neighbours does as much for morale as it does for hunger. Cooking for dozens of people is very different from cooking at home, though. It needs planning, careful food hygiene, a sense of what things cost, and real care for the people you are feeding, whatever they can and cannot eat. This section will cover cooking safely and fairly for a crowd.

## Can you help write this?

We are looking for people who cook for large numbers, such as community cafe and lunch club volunteers, school and canteen cooks, caterers, faith community kitchen teams and people trained in food hygiene. We would especially welcome people who can advise on allergies and on vegetarian, vegan, halal and kosher food. If that could be you, please [get in touch](/get-involved/).

## What we hope to cover

### Community cooking

- Planning a menu and quantities for a crowd
- Food hygiene and safety when cooking in bulk
- Equipment, kitchens and cooking without mains power
- Organising volunteers in the kitchen

### Understanding the economics

- Budgeting, and working out the cost per portion
- Buying in bulk and finding cheap, nutritious ingredients
- Cutting waste and using up leftovers safely
- Sharing costs fairly, and feeding people who cannot pay

### Allergies

- The 14 allergens that UK food law requires businesses to identify, and why they matter for community kitchens too
- Asking about allergies, and labelling food clearly
- Avoiding cross-contamination when cooking in bulk
- What to do if someone has a serious allergic reaction

### Dietary needs

- **Vegetarian and vegan.** Making meals everyone can share, and getting enough protein and nutrients.
- **Halal.** What it means, where to buy halal meat, and keeping it separate.
- **Kosher.** What it means, and when to ask for advice from the local Jewish community.
- **Other needs,** such as gluten-free, diabetic and soft diets, and other religious and cultural practices.

A simple rule that covers much of this: if in doubt, ask. People are usually glad to be asked what they can eat, and it is a good way to make everyone feel welcome at the table.
