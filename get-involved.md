# How can I get involved?

We want to build a small team of people who know their subject well. Together we will find the best existing how-to guides and resources, and write new ones where nothing good exists. Every guide should be practical, written in plain English and suited to life in the UK. You do not need to be a professional. If you have done something for years and could explain it to a neighbour, we would like to hear from you.

## What we would ask of you

- Point us to guides, books, websites or organisations you trust.
- Write or check a short guide on your subject, usually one or two pages.
- Answer the odd question from other contributors.

Give as much or as little time as suits you. We will credit you by name unless you would rather not be named. Everything we publish is free for others to copy and adapt under our [licence](/licence/), so your work can reach as many people as possible.

## Who we are looking for

### The practical side

- **Community organisers** who know how to bring neighbours together into groups, run a good meeting, set up a street contact list or phone tree, and keep volunteers involved over time.
- **Gardeners and food growers** who can advise on growing food in the UK climate, in gardens, allotments, window boxes and shared spaces, and on saving, storing and swapping seeds.
- **Cooks and food preservers** who know how to store food safely, preserve what you grow, and cook for a crowd on a budget.
- **People who run community larders or food banks**, who understand how to share food fairly and with dignity.
- **Radio amateurs and LoRa enthusiasts** who can explain how to keep people in touch when phones and the internet fail, including how to get licensed.
- **Electricians, heating engineers and energy advisers** who can explain how to keep warm safely in a power cut, and how to use backup power, solar panels and batteries.
- **People who look after community buildings**, such as meeting houses, churches, mosques, temples and village halls, and who know how to open them as warm spaces, with the right insurance, safeguarding and planning.
- **First aiders, nurses and mental health first aiders** who can help people look after each other in a crisis.
- **People with experience of welcoming refugees**, such as hosts, caseworkers, interpreters and English teachers.

### The harder work

- **Youth workers, sports coaches, mentors and maker space organisers** who give young people real responsibility and something worthwhile to put their energy into.
- **Mediators and facilitators**, including people trained in the Alternatives to Violence Project, restorative practice or nonviolent communication, who can teach others to handle conflict well.
- **Librarians, teachers and journalists** who can help people check what they read and spot false stories before they spread.
- **People who work on community cohesion**, tackling prejudice and making sure no one feels left out.
- **Debt, housing and employment advisers** who understand the money worries and unfairness that feed anger and conflict.
- **People involved in town twinning, language teaching and exchanges** who build friendships across borders.
- **Historians and storytellers** who can tell the stories of people who showed courage without weapons.

### Helping run the site

- **Writers and editors**, especially anyone who knows how to write in plain English.
- **Illustrators and designers** who can make guides clear and easy to follow.
- **Translators**, so that guides can reach people whose first language is not English.
- **Anyone comfortable with simple websites** who could help publish guides.

## Not on the list?

If you have a skill that would help a community look after itself and each other, please get in touch anyway. The list will grow as more people join.
