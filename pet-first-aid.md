# First aid for pets

In an emergency, vets may be hard to reach, and pets can be hurt, frightened or made ill by things that happen to the rest of the household: a fire, a flood, cold, heat, or food and medicines left within reach. A little knowledge can help you keep an animal safe and comfortable until you can get it to a vet.

We have not written this page yet, because we want it written or checked by someone who really knows the subject. If you are a vet, a veterinary nurse, or someone with long experience of caring for animals, please [get in touch](/get-involved/). We would love your help.

<p class="notice">If your pet is injured or seriously unwell, call your vet straight away. If your practice is closed, its phone line or website will usually tell you where the nearest out-of-hours vet is.</p>

## What we hope to cover

- **A pet first aid kit.** What to keep for dogs, cats and other common pets.
- **Common emergencies.** Bleeding, burns, choking, heatstroke, cold and being hit by a car.
- **Poisoning.** Common foods, plants, medicines and household products that are dangerous to pets, and what to do if your pet eats something it should not.
- **Handling an injured animal safely,** so that you do not get bitten or scratched by a frightened pet.
- **Keeping pets calm** during fireworks, storms, evacuations and other stressful times.
- **A pet emergency plan.** Food, medicines, records and carriers, who will care for your pets if you cannot, and keeping microchip details up to date.
- **Help with vet costs.** Charities that offer low-cost or free care to people who cannot afford it.
