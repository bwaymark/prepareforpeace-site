# Keeping in touch when the networks fail

Mobile phones and the internet depend on power and on masts that can go down. Radio does not need either. A few people on each street with a simple radio can pass on news, check on neighbours and call for help when nothing else works. This section covers anything with a transmitter, from licence-free walkie-talkies to amateur radio and low-cost mesh networks.

## Peacemakers on every channel

When the power goes off and the phones go quiet, people are frightened. Fear makes rumours spread quickly, and rumours soon look for someone to blame: a neighbour, a stranger, a group of people who seem different. History shows how fast that blame can turn into hostility, and hostility into violence. Violence between groups nearly always begins with words, long before anyone picks up a weapon.

That is why every channel needs peacemakers. On a radio net, a Meshtastic channel, a street noticeboard or a phone tree, a few calm voices can make the difference. A peacemaker checks a story before passing it on, says "we don't know that yet" when others are sure, and gently refuses to join in when people start to blame or dehumanise others. A peacemaker also brings accurate information, practical help and a reminder that the people being blamed are neighbours too.

Speaking up like this can leave you exposed. When tempers are high, the person who disagrees can become the next target. This is why a firm commitment to peace matters so much. Someone who is known not to threaten anyone, and who will not answer anger with anger, is far less likely to be met with violence. And if standing up for what is right still brings hardship, it is better to suffer for doing right than for doing wrong. As the Christian scriptures put it:

> "For it is better, if it is the will of God, to suffer for doing good than for doing evil."
>
> 1 Peter 3:17

People of many faiths and none share that conviction. Whatever your beliefs, if you are going to be on the air in a crisis, be one of the calm voices.
