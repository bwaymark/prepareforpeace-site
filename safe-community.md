# Community safety

The safest streets are not the ones with the most cameras. They are the ones where neighbours know each other, notice when something is wrong, and help without being asked. A community that looks out for its own is also far less likely to turn on outsiders, because people feel less alone and less afraid.

## Can you help write this?

We are looking for Neighbourhood Watch coordinators, community organisers, police community support officers and safeguarding leads who can share what works. If that could be you, please [get in touch](/get-involved/).

## What we hope to cover

- **Knowing your neighbours.** Simple ways to get to know the people on your street, and who might need a check in hard times.
- **Neighbourhood Watch.** Joining or starting a scheme, and how to do it in a way that welcomes people rather than watching them with suspicion.
- **Looking out for others.** Checking on people who live alone, are elderly or are unwell, without intruding.
- **Safe spaces.** Running a warm space or community hub safely, including basic safeguarding for children and vulnerable adults, and when volunteers need DBS checks.
- **Reporting concerns.** When to call the police on 999 or 101, and how to pass on worries about someone's welfare.
- **Calm in a crisis.** Stopping rumours and blame before they turn neighbours against each other.
